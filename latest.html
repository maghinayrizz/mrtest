Write it once, fix it once.

A field-by-field guide to improving your Dev Request before it's submitted — fewer rejections, shorter triage time, less back-and-forth between Development and Support.

We're launching Salesforce: Tips & Tricks, a series built to make your day-to-day in Salesforce easier.

First up: Dev Requests. This edition is here for one reason: to help you raise the quality of your request before you hit submit. Better requests mean fewer rejections, faster triage, and far less back-and-forth between the Development and Support teams.

Why requests get sent back

  • 1
    More information required The request doesn't yet give Dev enough to act on — a common, avoidable reason for a bounce-back.
  • 2
    Steps to reproduce are poorly written — or the issue can't be reproduced Engineers tend to just copy and paste from the case or what the customer has reported, rather than writing steps a developer can actually follow.

Field-by-field: what "good" looks like

A closer look at the fields that most often decide whether a request sails through or bounces back.

Found in PhaseFrequently reported incorrectly by engineers. It should reflect where the customer experienced the issue.
System RevisionNeeds to match the real, current application version.
Business Impact — describe what's happening now, not what could happen. Quantify it wherever you can, to help Dev determine priority.
✕"Could stop the customer from invoicing."
✓"Blocking the customer from invoicing — $100,000 in lost revenue."
✕"Operations halted. Can't build routes." (copied word-for-word into the Do Nothing Impact field too)
✓"Operations halted — unable to build routes for 30 customers."
Expected & Actual ResultsYou're expected to be able to describe or present the actual result.
Functional AreaPicking the wrong area delays triage by sending the request to the wrong team.
AttachmentsShow your analysis — database screenshots, error messages, SQL tables. This is the evidence that lets Dev pick up where you left off.
Repro StepsSteps need to be clearly outlined so a developer can reproduce the issue. It's okay if you can't reproduce it, as long as the impact is clearly discussed with sufficient evidence.
ⓘ On Priority 2: the assessment depends on the quality of the information provided in the request — but it needs to be accurate.
